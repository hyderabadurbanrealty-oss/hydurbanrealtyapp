Outlook
01. Is the current Hyderabad real estate market in a growth cycle, balanced phase, correction
phase, or stable stage?
According to our observations, the Hyderabad residential real estate market is currently moving through a healthy
correction phase.
This should not be viewed as a slowdown, but rather as a natural recalibration of the market after a strong growth
period over the past few years.
During the previous cycle, property values in several micro-markets witnessed rapid appreciation within a short span
of time, which created a temporary aordability gap for many end users.
The current phase is allowing the market to stabilize and align pricing with buyer sentiment and purchasing capacity.
What we are seeing now is a more rational and sustainable market environment where:
• Buyers are becoming more active again as aordability improves.
• Developers are focusing on value-driven oerings and realistic pricing.
• Genuine end-user demand is returning to the market.
This correction is therefore strengthening the long-term fundamentals of Hyderabad’s real estate sector, enabling
buyers to confidently explore and purchase their dream homes at more balanced price points.
From our perspective, phase is a positive structural adjustment that will support the next sustainable growth cycle for
the city’s real estate market.
Ravi Netha, Founder of HydUrban Realty, is a real estate advisor focused on the Hyderabad
residential market. Through HydUrban Realty, he provides research-driven guidance to
homebuyers and investors, closely tracking market trends, infrastructure developments,
and pricing dynamics across Hyderabad’s key growth corridors.
As part of this report, 1 Finance conducted a Q&A discussion with Ravi Netha to capture an

02. Which areas within Hyderabad do you believe are best positioned for steady growth over the
next few years, and how important is infrastructure in shaping that outlook?
The hyderabad western corridor continues to remain one of the strongest residential growth zones in the city.
Areas such as Kokapet, Neopolis, Financial District, Narsingi, Puppalaguda, and Nanakramguda benefit from their close
proximity to Hyderabad’s major IT and business districts.
These locations have already witnessed strong residential demand due to convenient access to workplaces,
well-developed social infrastructure, and the presence of several premium residential developments.
At the same time, emerging micro-markets such as Tellapur, Kollur, and Osman Nagar are gaining momentum due to
their strong connectivity through the Outer Ring Road (ORR) and their relative proximity to the Financial District and
the broader IT corridor.
These areas are witnessing the development of large gated communities and high-rise residential projects, supported
by improved road connectivity and growing interest from both homebuyers and developers.
Another important growth corridor that is increasingly attracting attention is the Budvel–Shamshabad belt. Budvel is
witnessing significant infrastructure-led development, including planned IT and mixed-use clusters, which are
expected to transform the area into a major residential and commercial hub in the coming years. Its proximity to the
Rajiv Gandhi International Airport, along with improved connectivity through the ORR and upcoming infrastructure
projects, is driving growing developer interest in this region.
The Shamshabad corridor is also seeing strong development activity, with several large developers such as Godrej,
Ramky, Prestige Group , large-scale residential and integrated township developments.
These projects are expected to create new residential clusters supported by commercial growth, hospitality
developments, and airport-driven economic activity in the southern part of Hyderabad.
Infrastructure continues to play a critical role in shaping the long-term outlook of these locations.
Connectivity through the Outer Ring Road, airport access, radial road expansions, and other planned infrastructure
projects is significantly influencing buyer preferences and developer investments.
As these infrastructure initiatives mature, they are expected to further strengthen the residential potential of these
emerging corridors.
Overall, from the standpoint thr locations that combine strong infrastructure connectivity, proximity to employment
hubs, and large-scale planned developments—particularly along the western corridor and the emerging
Budvel–Shamshabad growth belt—are likely to drive Hyderabad’s next phase of residential growth.

03. How does Hyderabad currently compare with Bengaluru in terms of pricing, demand
momentum, and long-term investment attractiveness? Do you see Hyderabad maintaining its
relative aordability advantage?
Both Hyderabad and Bengaluru are strong technology-driven real estate markets, but they dier in terms of market
maturity, infrastructure dynamics, and aordability positioning.
Bengaluru has historically been a more established technology hub with a mature real estate market and a long
track record of demand from the IT and startup ecosystem.
Hyderabad, on the other hand, has emerged very strongly over the past decade and is increasingly positioning itself
as a competitive alternative to Bengaluru, supported by rapid infrastructure development and expanding corporate
presence.
One of Hyderabad’s key advantages today is its planned infrastructure and connectivity. Wider roads, strong
connectivity through the Outer Ring Road, and expanding urban corridors have helped the city maintain relatively
smoother mobility compared to many other large metropolitan markets.
This has enhanced the overall livability experience and made Hyderabad increasingly attractive for professionals
relocating to the city.
Another important factor is the growing presence of Global Capability Centres (GCCs), IT companies, and
pharmaceutical firms, which continue to expand their operations in Hyderabad.
This steady influx of companies is driving employment growth and supporting sustained residential demand.
In terms of pricing, Hyderabad still oers relatively better value when compared to Bengaluru, particularly when
evaluating the scale of developments and the quality of lifestyle oerings.
The city is witnessing a significant shift toward premium gated communities and high-rise developments, with
several projects now reaching 40 to 50+ floors and transforming the city’s residential skyline.
This reflects a broader trend of homebuyers upgrading to higher-quality living environments and larger community
developments.
From a long-term investment perspective, Hyderabad remains highly attractive due to its infrastructure-led growth,
expanding employment base, and continued availability of land for planned developments. While Bengaluru will
continue to remain a major technology and residential market, Hyderabad is steadily strengthening its position and
is likely to retain a relative aordability advantage while oering strong potential for long-term capital appreciation.
Hyderabad today represents a compelling real estate market that combines infrastructure growth, livability
improvements, and strong housing demand, making it an increasingly attractive destination for both end-users and
long-term investors.

The value of residential sales across Hyderabad declined steadily through 2025 after peaking in Q1, mirroring the
slowdown in unit absorption. City-level transaction value rose from ₹25,303 crore in Q4 2024 to ₹29,251 crore in Q1
2025, before easing consistently to ₹20,977 crore in Q4 2025, indicating a more cautious buying environment in the
second half of the year.
Sales in Q4 2025 were heavily skewed toward larger configurations. 3/3.5 BHK units dominated absorption, with
5,275 units sold, followed by 2/2.5 BHK units at 2,451 units, indicating strong end-user preference for mid-to-large
homes. 4 BHK and larger units accounted for 2,055 sales, reflecting selective but meaningful demand for
premium configurations.
